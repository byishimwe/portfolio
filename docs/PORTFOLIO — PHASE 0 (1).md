# **00\. The Project in One Sentence**

Build a visually distinctive, professionally credible portfolio that demonstrates design taste and frontend-development ability, presents three strong projects, and converts interested visitors into potential web-design and development clients.

The portfolio is not simply a collection of past work.

**It is simultaneously a sales asset, a design statement, and a demonstration of execution quality.**

It needs to work for two audiences:

1. A business owner evaluating whether to hire us.  
2. A designer, developer, or creative professional evaluating our craft.

The first audience determines our commercial priorities. The second raises the standard of execution.

# **06\. The Signature Concept — The Living Frame**

## **6.1 Definition**

**The Living Frame is an editorial gallery where the work changes the surrounding atmosphere while the visual language remains continuous.**

The visitor moves between projects through one recognizable system of framing, composition, typography, and controlled transitions.

## **6.2 Creative balance**

**70% Editorial Exhibition / 30% Cinematic Camera**

Editorial characteristics:

* Precise grids  
* Disciplined spacing  
* Refined typography  
* Strong image framing  
* Intentional alignment  
* Quiet hierarchy

Cinematic characteristics:

* Layered depth  
* Masked reveals  
* Gentle image movement  
* Atmospheric shifts  
* Spatial continuity

## **6.3 The intended emotional impression**

The visitor should feel like they are moving through a carefully curated exhibition of work.

Each project introduces its own character without destroying the portfolio's overall identity.

## **6.4 Essential behavioral rules**

1. The work must remain understandable without motion.  
2. The gallery must never prevent visitors from accessing project links.  
3. A visitor must be able to identify the available projects quickly.  
4. Motion must not block ordinary scrolling or interaction.  
5. Mobile behavior may differ from desktop behavior.  
6. Reduced-motion preferences must be respected.  
7. Project imagery must remain the visual focus.  
8. Other animations must be quieter than the gallery's primary transition.

## **6.5 Same-day implementation levels**

### **Level A — Launch baseline**

A refined gallery with a consistent editorial frame and excellent static or lightly animated presentation.

All three projects are visible or easily discoverable.

This level must work even without JavaScript-powered scroll choreography.

### **Level B — Minimum viable Living Frame**

Subtle transitions between projects, including:

* Crossfades or masked reveals  
* Limited image scaling  
* Controlled atmosphere changes  
* Smooth metadata transitions

No unusual navigation or scroll hijacking.

**Target for today if baseline functionality is complete.**

### **Level C — Full signature enhancement**

Potentially includes:

* Advanced pinned gallery choreography  
* More elaborate responsive composition changes  
* Spatially continuous transitions into case studies  
* FLIP or View Transition techniques  
* Sophisticated frame-to-page animation

**Not a launch blocker.**

These levels preserve the creative vision without making the advanced implementation a dependency for outreach.

# **09\. Visual Direction — Strategic Commitments**

Exact design tokens belong to Phase 2\.

However, Phase 0 locks the general direction.

Editorial, architectural, restrained, cinematic, premium.

## **9.2 Base palette**

Predominantly warm off-white, charcoal, and carefully controlled neutral tones.

Large, expressive display typography balanced by compact, highly legible supporting text.

* Strong hierarchy  
* Generous but purposeful negative space  
* Oversized project imagery

* Futuristic and sci-fi motifs  
* Neon tech aesthetics  
* Generic glassmorphism  
* Overuse of gradients  
* Excessive decorative shapes  
* Crowded dashboards of technical skills  
* Constant animations  
* Interactions that require instructions

# **14\. Risks and Mitigations**

## **Risk 1 — Overdesigning**

**Problem:** Too much time is spent exploring references and visual variants.

**Mitigation:** Select one visual system in Phase 2\. No prolonged concept competition.

## **Risk 2 — Signature interaction delays deployment**

**Problem:** Scroll choreography or shared-element transitions create debugging overhead.

**Mitigation:** Implement a stable gallery first. Keep the advanced layer isolated.

## **Risk 3 — Weak project imagery**

**Problem:** Good projects look underwhelming because screenshots are poor or poorly composed.

**Mitigation:** Prioritize one convincing real image per project before creating extensive galleries.

## **Risk 4 — Unclear offer**

**Problem:** The visitor admires the design but cannot tell what service is being sold.

**Mitigation:** Use explicit service language in the hero and services section.

## **Risk 5 — Fictional projects appear misleading**

**Problem:** Concept work is described as commissioned business work.

**Mitigation:** Use visible, tactful concept-project identification and accurate descriptions.

## **Risk 6 — Broken contact path**

**Problem:** A prospect wants to make contact but cannot reach us.

**Mitigation:** Manually verify WhatsApp and email destinations on a real device.

## **Risk 7 — Mobile compromises**

**Problem:** Desktop effects produce an awkward or slow smartphone experience.

**Mitigation:** Design a deliberately simpler mobile presentation.

## **Risk 8 — Outreach starts too late**

**Problem:** Continuous refinements consume time reserved for contacting prospects.

**Mitigation:** Deploy once the P0 checklist passes; improvements are made afterward.

# **15\. Acceptance Criteria**

## **15.1 Commercial**

The site passes if a first-time visitor can quickly answer:

* Who created this?  
* What does this person offer?  
* What kind of work can they produce?  
* Where can I see examples?  
* How do I contact them?

## **15.2 Visual**

The site passes if:

* Typography feels deliberate and consistent.  
* Projects are visually convincing.  
* There is a recognizable visual identity.  
* Layouts remain composed at desktop, tablet, and mobile widths.  
* Motion supports rather than overwhelms the work.

## **15.3 Functional**

The site passes if:

* Every published navigation and project link works.  
* WhatsApp and email links work.  
* All three project entries display correctly.  
* No core content is lost without animation.  
* Keyboard navigation is usable.  
* Reduced-motion preferences are respected.  
* No obvious overflow or broken layout appears at common screen widths.  
* Reloading the public URL works.

## **15.4 Content**

The site passes if:

* All project descriptions accurately reflect real implementations.  
* Concept work is identified correctly.  
* No placeholder copy remains.  
* No unverified achievements appear.  
* All critical images are genuine representations of the work.

## **15.5 Launch**

The site passes if:

* A public URL is available.  
* At least one desktop and one mobile review have been completed.  
* Essential contact links have been manually tested.  
* A prospect can reach the strongest work without unnecessary interaction.  
* The URL is ready to include in outreach messages.
